# Case 7.1 (United States): Healthcare — Kaiser Permanente's ambient AI scribe saved nearly 16,000 documentation hours in 15 months

**Industry / scenario**: (Large enterprise: The Permanente Medical Group, tool available to 10,000 physicians across 21 Northern California medical centers) Outpatient visit documentation.

**Pain point**: Clinical documentation is a leading driver of physician burnout — notes and orders are often finished after hours ("pajama time"), hurting job satisfaction.

**AI approach**: Launched in late 2023: with patient consent, a secure smartphone microphone transcribes (but does not record) the visit and drafts the note; the physician reviews and edits before filing. The tool makes no diagnoses and offers no treatment recommendations.

**Outcome**: From Oct 2023 to Dec 2024, 7,260 physicians used it across 2,576,627 outpatient visits, saving an estimated 15,791 hours of documentation (~1,794 eight-hour days). Users' note time, per-visit duration and after-hours EHR use all fell significantly; the heaviest users benefited most. In a 102-physician survey, 84% said it improved visits (a KP research press release says 88%); among 118 patients surveyed, 47% said the doctor looked at the screen less. During the 2023 10-week pilot (3,442 physicians, 303,266 visits), most users spent ~1 hour less per day at the computer. Early evaluation of 35 transcripts averaged 48/50, with rare hallucinations. (Provenance: institution self-reported, published by Kaiser Permanente and its research division; study in NEJM Catalyst; AMA paraphrase.)

**Firm profile**: Hospital groups, specialty clinics and telehealth providers with heavy documentation burdens and after-hours charting; requires patient consent flows and physician review processes.
