# Case 24.6 (United Kingdom / Hong Kong, China, FAILURE CASE): Construction & Engineering — An Arup employee in Hong Kong falls for an AI deepfake video-conference scam as internal controls fail, transferring HK\$200 million

**Industry / scenario**: (Large enterprise: 18,500 employees across 34 offices worldwide) Arup (奥雅纳), a London-headquartered engineering design and consulting firm. The scenario is finance payment approval. Note: this is a case of a company employee being defrauded with AI deepfake technology and internal controls failing, not a failure of an AI project of Arup's own.

**Pain point**: This was not an AI project that Arup initiated. The incident occurred in the finance payment process: the employee involved was a finance staff member at Arup Hong Kong, and the fraudsters first used an email that appeared to come from the UK office to propose a "secret transaction."

**Why it failed**: According to Hong Kong police, a finance employee at Arup Hong Kong first received an email that appeared to come from the UK office; because the email asked for a "secret transaction" to be carried out, he initially suspected it was a phishing email. He then joined a video conference in which he believed the participants were the company's chief financial officer and other colleagues; in fact, all of them were deepfake images. CNN defines deepfakes as "fake videos that have been created using artificial intelligence." Arup confirmed that fake voices and images were used in the scam.

**Outcome**: The employee agreed to transfer a total of HK\$200 million (about US\$25.6 million), sent in 15 transactions according to RTHK citing police. Arup reported the case to Hong Kong police in January 2024, and said the company's financial stability and business operations were not affected and its internal systems were not compromised. Rob Greig, the company's global chief information officer, said the number and sophistication of such attacks had risen sharply in recent months.

**Lessons**: Generative AI means that seeing and hearing the boss on a video call no longer proves the other party is who they appear to be. Large payments must not be released on the strength of a single video conference alone; they need independent verification channels outside the meeting (for example, calling back a known number, or multi-person approval), and finance staff need dedicated training showing them what AI deepfake scams look like.
