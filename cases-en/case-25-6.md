# Case 25.6 (Australia, FAILURE CASE): Tourism — A small tour company outsources marketing blog posts written with AI, which invent a nonexistent "Weldborough Hot Springs"; tourists make the trip only to find nothing there

**Industry / scenario**: (Small company: Euronews describes it as a smaller, boutique-type operator; the operator says it is a husband-and-wife business that employs sales staff; headcount not disclosed) Tour operator Tasmania Tours, run by Australian Tours and Cruises of New South Wales. The scenario is destination marketing content on the company website's blog.

**Pain point**: The operator said the company has to compete with big companies, so its website content must be constantly updated, but "We don't have enough horsepower to write enough content on our own," so it outsourced this part of the work.

**Why it failed**: According to the operator's own account: the company did not have enough staff to write enough content itself, so it outsourced marketing content to a third party that used AI; the company has to compete with big companies. He said he normally reviews every post before publication, but this blog post was published while he was overseas, and some posts went live by mistake.

**Outcome**: The blog post "7 Best Hot Springs Tasmania Experiences for 2026," published in July 2025, urged tourists to visit "Weldborough Hot Springs," described as "a peaceful escape," but these hot springs do not exist. The owner of the local Weldborough Hotel said that tourists began asking about the hot springs from September; she was receiving about 5 phone calls a day, and at least two or three people a day were coming to the hotel looking for the hot springs; according to Euronews, she also said that two days earlier a group of 24 drivers had made the trip specifically. The blog post has been deleted. The operator admitted that "our AI has messed up completely."

**Lessons**: When a small business outsources content to a third party that uses AI, it still cannot skip the step of having a person verify the content before publication, and when that person is away, a backup reviewer is all the more necessary. Once verifiable facts such as locations and facilities are wrong, they directly affect tourists' travel and also damage the company's reputation.

*Sources: Euronews, Craig Saueurs, "How an AI-generated travel website sent tourists to a non-existent hot spring attraction" (2026-01-30) (media report); The New Daily, "Tourists flock to Tasmanian hot springs, only to find an AI hallucination" (2026-02-02) (media report, includes statements from the operator and the local hotel owner)*
