# Case 7.3 (United States): Healthcare — five health systems' controlled study on AI scribes: ~16 fewer documentation minutes per day, benefits depend on usage depth

**Industry / scenario**: (Size undisclosed: five large U.S. health systems including a public academic system; 8,581 outpatient clinicians in the study) Outpatient clinicians at Mass General Brigham, UCSF, Emory Healthcare, Yale New Haven Health and UC Davis using ambient AI documentation assistants.

**Pain point**: AI scribes were being heavily promoted; hospitals needed objective multi-center data on how much documentation time they actually save.

**AI approach**: The systems deployed ambient AI scribes on varying schedules. The research team (Ambient Clinical Documentation Collaborative) used a difference-in-differences design comparing EHR usage data of 1,800+ users against 6,770 non-users at the same institutions (June 2023 – Aug 2025).

**Outcome**: Per the JAMA study (2026-04-01), users cut total EHR time by ~13 minutes per 8-hour outpatient day (~3% relative) and documentation time by ~16 minutes (~10% relative); after-hours EHR use showed no significant difference; ~0.5 more visits per week and ~$167 more per user per month (the researchers call this "statistically significant but small"). Physicians using the tool in >50% of visits saw roughly 2× the EHR-time reduction and 3× the documentation-time reduction — but only ~32% of users reached that frequency. Primary care physicians, advanced practice providers and women clinicians benefited more; surgical specialties saw no significant change. (Provenance: independent academic research published via an MGB press release; MGB's own headline calls the reduction "modest"; minute figures are written as "per day" in the MGB release.)

**Firm profile**: Hospital groups and large clinic networks planning at-scale purchases of AI documentation tools — and procurement teams that need objective data to calibrate vendor claims and set usage-rate targets.

*Source: Mass General Brigham press release "AI Scribes Linked to Modest Reductions in Electronic Health Record Use and Clinical Documentation Time" (2026-04-01), paraphrasing the JAMA study "Changes in Clinician Time Expenditure and Visit Quantity With Adoption of Artificial Intelligence-Powered Scribes: A Multisite Study" (independent research, institution-published)*
