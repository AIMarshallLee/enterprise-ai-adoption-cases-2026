# Case 40.1 (Australia): Software & IT services — ANZ ran a randomized controlled trial before rolling out GitHub Copilot; task completion time down ~42%

**Industry / scenario**: (Large enterprise: ANZ has 5,000+ engineers) Internal software engineering teams at a bank. Scenario: a controlled experiment before at-scale rollout of an AI coding assistant.

**Pain point**: With AI coding tools spreading, ANZ wanted to evaluate GitHub Copilot in a controlled setting against real engineering tasks, covering efficiency, code quality and security.

**AI approach**: A 6-week experiment (2 weeks setup, 4 weeks testing) from mid-June to end-July 2023. 100+ engineers were randomized: the Copilot group had the plugin enabled; the control group had it disabled.

**Outcome**: The paper reports Copilot lifted ANZ engineers' productivity by 42.36% on average measured by task completion time (42.34% by the alternative calculation elsewhere in the paper). The Copilot group's method success rate was 12.86% higher, but the paper itself calls this statistically insignificant. The experiment failed to produce usable data on code security. Follow-up early data from ~1,000 engineers also showed clear productivity and satisfaction gains. (Provenance: research paper written by ANZ employees, arXiv preprint, noted as published in SEC 2024 proceedings; The Register paraphrased it.)

**Firm profile**: Engineering-heavy, compliance-driven organizations (like banks) that want data — not demos — to decide on at-scale AI coding tool rollout.

*Source: arXiv "The Impact of AI Tool on Engineering at ANZ Bank — An Empirical Study on GitHub Copilot within Corporate Environment" (2024-02-08, Sayan Chatterjee et al.) (employee-authored research)*
