# Case 19.5 (United States): Public safety — a 26-staff county dispatch center routes non-emergency calls to AI; non-emergency volume down ~75%

**Industry / scenario**: (Public institution: MACC 911's 2020 job posting lists 26 staff with a $4.8M budget) MACC 911 is the consolidated dispatch center for Grant County, Washington, serving 30 agencies including 9 law-enforcement, 16 fire and 2 EMS organizations. Scenario: answering, triaging and information-gathering for non-emergency calls.

**Pain point**: The vendor states ~60% of call volume was non-emergency. MACC 911's technology services manager says the industry suffers chronic turnover, burnout and understaffing.

**AI approach**: Aurelian's AI voice assistant answers non-emergency lines, triages and collects information, generating incident records for dispatcher review; emergencies ring through to human call-takers.

**Outcome**: The vendor reports total call volume down ~50% and non-emergency volume down ~75%; ~15,000 calls handled automatically in the first two months, generating ~7,000 incident records, with only 20% of those callers reaching a human; calls that took 3+ minutes became ~30-second reviews (vendor case page, undated). As of Oct 2024, AVA had handled 30,000+ interactions, saving dispatchers 1,500+ hours of call time (media coverage of a MACC 911 statement). A Dec 2025 board meeting minute records the 100,000th non-emergency call handled (official record). SNO911's Oct 2024 presentation says the system auto-handles ~75–80% of MACC 911's non-emergency calls — a slightly different figure from the vendor's "~75% reduction," given side by side. (Provenance: vendor-reported, media-reported and official minutes, as labeled.)

**Firm profile**: Consolidated emergency-dispatch centers and public-safety agencies drowning in non-emergency calls, where emergency lines must stay human-staffed.
