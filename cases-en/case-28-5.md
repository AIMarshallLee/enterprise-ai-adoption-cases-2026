# Case 28.5 (United States, FAILURE CASE): Agriculture — Bowery Farming, a vertical farm marketed on computer vision and machine learning, ceases operations after raising more than US\$700 million

**Industry / scenario**: (Size undisclosed: at least 187 layoffs at shutdown according to WARN notices; more than US\$700 million raised in total) Indoor vertical farming (controlled-environment agriculture), producing leafy greens. According to 2018 reporting, Bowery's various hydroponic systems were connected through its in-house software BoweryOS, which used computer vision and robotic automation, guided by machine-learning algorithms, to monitor crops and the variables affecting their growth around the clock; investor General Catalyst at the time saw this emphasis on software and AI as a key differentiator.

**Pain point**: Bowery ran indoor vertical farms and sold pesticide-free leafy-green products. According to 2018 reporting, its in-house software platform was very attractive to venture capital, and investors saw its emphasis on software and AI as a key differentiator. The sources do not explain in more specific terms what business problem the company was using AI to solve.

**Why it failed** (only what the sources explicitly state; the sources do not attribute the failure to the AI technology itself):

- AgNavigator: difficulty scaling up profitably; high capital expenditure to build and equip vertical farms; failure to obtain new funding or find a buyer. Labor accounts for a quarter to a third of a vertical farm's expenses, and although the company worked to automate its processes, labor costs may still have been one of the factors.
- Agriculture Dive: rising costs and a lack of venture funding; in early 2022 it borrowed US\$150 million in debt from KKR, and according to Axios this debt has since weighed heavily on the company.
- Vertical Farm Daily (relaying several former employees cited by PitchBook): in May 2023, one farm was hit by phytophthora, which destroyed a large amount of product and spread to another facility; any product that had been exposed to the pathogen could not be sold.
- The Baltimore Banner (citing Axios): several vertical farms, including Bowery, were struggling to stay afloat, as consumers were unwilling to pay more for products that are more sustainable but cost more to grow.

**Outcome**: In November 2024, according to PitchBook, Bowery ceased operations. According to WARN notices, 104 people were laid off in Bethlehem, Pennsylvania, and 83 people were laid off with the closure of the facility in Nottingham, Maryland. The company raised more than US\$700 million in total; there are two accounts of its valuation: the TechCrunch article text says its last round in 2021 valued it at more than US\$2 billion, while the summary on the same page and AgNavigator say US\$2.3 billion.

**Lessons**: AI and automation were at the core of Bowery's marketing, but the reasons for the shutdown listed in the sources are all operational: asset-heavy expansion, a funding gap, debt, crop disease, and consumers' unwillingness to pay a premium. The sources do not assess whether the AI systems themselves performed well. For business owners: AI can help with monitoring and management, but it cannot change the basic arithmetic of unit costs being higher than the price the market is willing to pay.
