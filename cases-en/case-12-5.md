# Case 12.5 (Japan): Hospitality — a 110-staff Hakone hot-spring inn in a government AI pilot: staff Q&A from 2 minutes to 10 seconds, scheduling from 30 minutes to 10 seconds

**Industry / scenario**: (Mid-size: Japan Tourism Agency report states 110 employees (2023 actual); recruitment listing says 120 (Jan 2026)) Hotel Okada, a long-established hot-spring inn in Hakone-Yumoto with 122 rooms. Scenario: internal staff Q&A, scheduling and guest-service preparation.

**Pain point**: Per the Tourism Agency pilot report: internal knowledge is scattered or missing, so new and transferred staff can't find accurate information; booking data alone can't tell whether to accept an order, so most staff check with managers, delaying decisions; guest data is scattered across PMS, surveys and daily reports, so repeat-guest information goes unused; reviews are watched but never analyzed for lack of time and method.

**AI approach**: In the Tourism Agency's FY2024 generative-AI pilot, Hotel Okada set four use cases: ① staff ask a bot inside LINE WORKS and generative AI answers from internal know-how and manuals; ② booking data feeds ML demand forecasts visualizing congestion by facility and dining venue to optimize scheduling; ③ scattered guest data is consolidated and RAG lets generative AI analyze surveys and PMS data, surfacing guest preferences; ④ auto-drafted replies to inquiries.

**Outcome**: Staff Q&A from ~2 minutes to ~10 seconds per question; shift scheduling from ~30 minutes to ~10 seconds (from the pilot report). (Provenance: government pilot report plus vendor-side announcements.)

**Firm profile**: Inns and hotels with 100+ staff where institutional knowledge sits in veterans' heads and guest data is fragmented across systems — a government-pilot pattern other operators can replicate.
