# Case 7.4 (Japan): Healthcare — A 111-bed community hospital uses on-premises generative AI to write nursing summaries; the vendor says time spent falls 54.2% from the 3rd use onward

**Industry / scenario**: (Mid-sized company: a social medical corporation with 111 general beds; hospital introduction material on the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare website lists 280 staff — that material dates from around FY2013, when the corporation was named a 特定医療法人 (tokutei iryō hōjin, specified medical corporation); a recruitment website lists 725 employees, undated) 社会医療法人祐愛会織田病院 (Shakai Iryō Hōjin Yūaikai Oda Byōin; Oda Hospital) in Kashima City, Saga Prefecture, which the paper describes as a regional core hospital in Japan. The scenarios are nurses writing nursing summaries (看護サマリー, kango samarī) at admission and discharge, and physicians writing medical information provision documents (referral letters).

**Pain point**: The vendor press release says that, in addition to treating patients, physicians and nurses must spend time writing medical records and reports, and that nursing summaries at admission and discharge and referral letters are a heavy burden among these; other companies' LLM services on the market require connecting in-hospital systems to the internet, and at hospitals that place particular emphasis on protecting personal information, meeting security requirements carries a cost, so adoption in clinical settings has been slow. The paper also states that paperwork has become a major burden for nurses.

**AI solution**: OPTiM developed OPTiM AI ホスピタル (OPTiM AI Hospital): it runs an LLM in an on-premises (オンプレミス) environment inside the hospital that requires no connection to the external internet at all, connects to シーエスアイ (CSI)'s electronic medical record system MI・RA・Is (ミライズ, Miraizu), and automatically generates drafts of admission and discharge nursing summaries and referral letters from EMR information. It was introduced at Oda Hospital from April to October 2024. The Department of General Medicine at Saga University Hospital (佐賀大学医学部附属病院 総合診療部), Oda Hospital and OPTiM conducted the research as an industry–academia–medical collaboration, which the paper describes as phased development and implementation; according to the paper's conflict-of-interest statement, the system has been provided to Oda Hospital as a product since 2025.

**Results**:

- Vendor press release (as of October 2024): average time per discharge nursing summary was 16.31 minutes before introduction; 10.90 minutes on first use (a 33.2% reduction); and 7.47 minutes from the 3rd use onward (a 54.2% reduction). (vendor-reported)
- Academic paper: median summary time fell from 16 minutes to 10 minutes, a reduction of about 40% (p\<0.001); limitations stated by the authors include a single hospital, assessments that could not be blinded, and a sample size that was not determined in advance. Two of the authors are OPTiM employees. (academic research; authors include vendor employees)

**Who this fits**: Regional hospitals of roughly 100–300 beds that are wary of patient data leaving the hospital and whose nurses carry heavy documentation burdens; hospitals that want to start with one type of fixed-format document (discharge summaries, referral letters).
