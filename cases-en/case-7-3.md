# Case 7.3 (United States): Healthcare — A controlled study of AI scribes at five health systems: about 16 fewer minutes of documentation a day, with effects depending on depth of use

**Industry / scenario**: (Size undisclosed: five large U.S. health systems, including public university-affiliated health systems, with a combined 8,581 ambulatory clinicians in the study) Ambulatory clinicians at Mass General Brigham, UCSF, Emory Healthcare, Yale New Haven Health and UC Davis using ambient AI scribes.

**Pain point**: AI scribes are being heavily promoted, and hospitals need multicenter objective data to judge how much documentation time they actually save.

**AI solution**: The institutions progressively deployed ambient AI scribes. The research team (Ambient Clinical Documentation Collaborative) used a difference-in-differences design to compare objective EHR usage data for more than 1,800 users with that of 6,770 non-users at the same institutions (data period: June 2023 – August 2025).

**Results**: According to the study published in JAMA (2026-04-01), users' total EHR time fell by about 13 minutes per 8 hours of scheduled clinic time (a 3% relative reduction), and documentation time fell by about 16 minutes (a 10% relative reduction); there was no significant difference in after-hours EHR use; users saw about 0.5 more clinic visits per week, bringing in about USD 167 more per user per month (which the researchers described as statistically significant but small in amount). Physicians who used the tool in more than 50% of visits saw about 2 times the reduction in total EHR time and about 3 times the reduction in documentation time, but only about 32% of users reached that frequency. Primary care physicians, advanced practice providers (APPs) and female clinicians benefited more; surgical specialties showed no significant change. (Basis: independent academic research, released via a Mass General Brigham press release; the MGB headline calls the reductions "modest"; the MGB press release presents the minute figures as daily.)

**Who this fits**: Hospital groups and large clinic networks planning to buy AI scribe tools at scale, and procurement decision-makers who need objective data to calibrate vendor claims and set usage-rate targets.

*Sources: Mass General Brigham press release, "AI Scribes Linked to Modest Reductions in Electronic Health Record Use and Clinical Documentation Time" (2026-04-01, relaying the JAMA study "Changes in Clinician Time Expenditure and Visit Quantity With Adoption of Artificial Intelligence-Powered Scribes: A Multisite Study") (independent research, released by the institution); [Telehealth.org](http://Telehealth.org), "Large JAMA Study Finds AI Scribes Cut Documentation Time for Ambulatory Clinicians" (media report)*
