# Case 6.1 (United States): Logistics — UPS's ORION routing optimization is projected to cut 100 million driving miles per year

**Industry / scenario**: (Large enterprise: ORION at full deployment covers 55,000 U.S. drivers) UPS; daily route planning for last-mile pickup and delivery.

**Pain point**: UPS had over a century of pickup-and-delivery practice to reconcile with new technology; early operations-research routing algorithms worked in the lab but failed to stick in real operations.

**AI approach**: ORION (On-Road Integrated Optimization and Navigation) was built on UPS's Package Flow Technology data platform — operations-research optimization, not generative AI or machine learning — and deployment began in 2012 after ~10 years of development. Using customer, driver and vehicle data plus the day's scheduled stops, it computes the most efficient route; a 120-stop route analyzes 200,000+ alternatives. Each morning drivers get an optimized delivery sequence.

**Outcome**: By December 2015, 35,000+ of 55,000 U.S. drivers used ORION, with full deployment planned for end-2016 (160 customers per driver per day). Deployed routes average 6–8 fewer miles each. At full deployment UPS projected 100M fewer miles per year, 10M gallons of fuel saved, 100,000 metric tons of CO₂ avoided, and $300–400M annual savings; $320M+ had been saved by Dec 2015 against ~$250M full-deployment cost. (Provenance: company self-reported — UPS press releases and INFORMS Edelman Award materials; annual figures are projections. ORION is operations-research optimization, not ML or generative AI.)

**Firm profile**: Parcel, retail-delivery and field-service operators with large fleets, many drivers and dense daily stops — provided a stops/vehicles/orders data platform exists first.
