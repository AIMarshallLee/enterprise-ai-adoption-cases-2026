# Case 5.2 (Germany): Automotive — AI predictive maintenance of final-assembly conveyor systems using existing control data avoids about 500 minutes of line stoppage a year

**Industry / scenario**: (Large enterprise: BMW Group, 154,540 employees as of end-2025) BMW Group Plant Regensburg; predictive maintenance of the final assembly line's conveyor systems.

**Pain point**: Up to 1,000 BMW 1 Series, X1 and X2 vehicles roll off the line at Plant Regensburg every working day. Any technical fault in the conveyor system can bring the final assembly line to a standstill.

**AI solution**: The monitoring system requires no additional sensors or hardware; instead, it analyzes existing data from installed components and the conveyor units' control systems, which is uploaded to the BMW Group's own predictive-maintenance cloud platform. Algorithms continuously search for anomalies such as fluctuations in current consumption; the system uses machine learning models developed in-house by BMW, which mark different anomalies on heat maps in different colours. When an anomaly is detected, the maintenance control centre receives an alert and assigns it to the maintenance technician on shift; the affected conveyor unit can be removed from the final assembly line and repaired offline. BMW has registered two patents for this in-house technology.

**Results**: The press release says that in vehicle final assembly at Plant Regensburg alone, the AI-supported system avoids an average of around 500 minutes of line stoppage per year; project manager Oliver Mrasek put it this way: "we are currently avoiding at least 500 minutes of downtime per year in vehicle assembly alone." About 80% of the main assembly line is already monitored this way, and the system is also used for the conveyor systems at Plants Dingolfing, Leipzig and Berlin. (Basis: company self-reported — BMW Group press release; the same release describes the 500 minutes both as an average of around 500 and as at least 500, and both are given here.)

**Who this fits**: Discrete manufacturers with fast cycle times, costly line stoppages and a lot of conveyor equipment — especially plants whose equipment control systems already generate data but that do not want to retrofit sensors on a large scale.

*Sources: BMW Group press release, "Smart maintenance using artificial intelligence" (2023-11-27); size: BMW Group website, "The Company" (company self-reported)*
