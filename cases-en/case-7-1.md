# Case 7.1 (United States): Healthcare — Kaiser Permanente physicians use an ambient AI documentation assistant, saving nearly 16,000 hours of documentation time in 15 months

**Industry / scenario**: (Large enterprise: The Permanente Medical Group; the tool was made available to 10,000 physicians across 21 medical centers in Northern California) A physician group within Kaiser Permanente in the U.S.; documentation of outpatient visits.

**Pain point**: Clinical documentation has long been one of the main causes of physician burnout; physicians often catch up on notes and orders outside normal working hours (so-called pajama time), which hurts job satisfaction.

**AI solution**: An ambient AI documentation assistant was launched in late 2023: with the patient's consent, it transcribes (but does not record) the visit conversation via a secure smartphone microphone and generates a draft note, which the physician reviews and edits before it is filed; the tool does not make diagnoses or give treatment recommendations.

**Results**: From October 2023 to December 2024, a total of 7,260 physicians used it in 2,576,627 outpatient visits, saving an estimated 15,791 hours of documentation time (about 1,794 8-hour workdays). Users' note time, time per visit, and EHR use outside 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. all fell significantly, with the heaviest users benefiting most. In a survey of 102 physicians, 84% said it had a positive effect on visits (a KP research division press release gives 88% instead); in a survey of 118 patients, 47% said their physician spent less time looking at the computer screen. During the 10-week pilot in 2023 (3,442 physicians, 303,266 outpatient visits), most physicians who used it spent on average about 1 hour less per day at the computer. An early evaluation of 35 transcript samples gave an average score of 48/50, with few hallucination events. (Basis: institution self-reported — published by Kaiser Permanente and its research division, with the study published in NEJM Catalyst; the AMA articles are secondary reporting.)

**Who this fits**: Hospital groups, specialty clinics and telehealth providers whose physicians carry heavy documentation burdens and do a lot of after-hours charting; patient notification or consent and a physician review process need to be put in place alongside the tool.
