# Case 12.5 (Japan): Hotels — Hotel Okada (ホテルおかだ), a 110-employee hot-spring ryokan in Hakone, joins a Japan Tourism Agency generative AI pilot: staff Q&A drops from 2 minutes to 10 seconds per query, and shift scheduling from 30 minutes to 10 seconds per run

**Industry / scenario**: (Mid-sized company: the Japan Tourism Agency report lists 110 employees (2023 actual); the recruitment site Mynavi (マイナビ) lists 120 (January 2026)) Hotel Okada Co., Ltd. (株式会社ホテルおかだ), a long-established hot-spring ryokan in Hakone-Yumoto, Kanagawa Prefecture, with 122 guest rooms. The scenario is the ryokan's internal staff Q&A, shift scheduling and preparation for receiving guests.

**Pain point**: According to the Japan Tourism Agency pilot report: in-house experience and know-how were scattered or insufficient, so new and transferred staff could not find accurate information; it was hard to tell from reservation information whether a booking could be accepted, most staff had to check with a superior, and decisions were often delayed; guest data was scattered across the PMS, questionnaires and daily reports, so information on repeat guests could not be put to use; staff routinely looked at review scores but had neither the time nor the method to analyze what the reviews actually said.

**AI solution**: In the Japan Tourism Agency's FY2024 generative AI survey project, Hotel Okada set four use cases: (1) staff ask questions through a bot on LINE WORKS, which was already in internal use, and generative AI answers automatically by referring to in-house know-how and manuals; (2) reservation data is aggregated and machine learning automatically generates demand forecasts, visualizing how busy the whole property and each dining venue will be, to optimize staff scheduling; (3) scattered guest data is consolidated, and generative AI uses RAG to analyze and summarize guest questionnaires and PMS data, surfacing guests' preferences and needs to staff; (4) reviews are collected automatically, and generative AI outputs web-based analysis reports by guest room and stay plan, which are used to improve the plans.

**Results**: The report's summary table shows: (1) internal Q&A dropped from 2 minutes to 10 seconds per instance (10 to 20 times a day); (2) shift scheduling dropped from 30 minutes to 10 seconds per run (once a day); (3) compiling guest information for receiving guests dropped from 5 minutes to 10 seconds per instance, and its frequency went from 10 to 15 times a day to once a day; (4) review analysis is a new task, so there is currently no reduction in work hours. Among people who had used generative AI across the 6 pilot regions nationwide, about 93% said they wanted to keep using it (N=27; this is not a figure for Hotel Okada alone). In August 2025, Hotel Okada and TripX started another Japan Tourism Agency pilot (automatic email replies with generative AI, and facility management); no results have been published yet. (Basis: government — the Japan Tourism Agency pilot report, compiled from the project report of the contractor Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu LLC; the report states that the evaluation method was interviews with users during the pilot and a questionnaire at its end, but does not say whether the work-hour figures in the summary table were measured or estimated, and there are no long-term operating data; the TripX part is vendor-reported.)

**Who this fits**: Regional hot-spring ryokan and small-to-mid-sized hotels with around a hundred staff and around a hundred guest rooms, where in-house knowledge is handed down by veteran staff and scheduling relies on experience; they can start with two use cases: an internal-manual Q&A bot and scheduling based on reservation data.
