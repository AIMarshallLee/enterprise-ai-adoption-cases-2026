# Case 38.6 (United States, FAILURE CASE): Legal services — New York lawyers submitted ChatGPT-invented case citations and were sanctioned $5,000 for bad faith

**Industry / scenario**: (Size undisclosed: headcount not given in sources) Levidow, Levidow & Oberman P.C., New York. Scenario: legal research and drafting for litigation.

**Pain point**: The firm mainly practices in New York state courts; its research tool Fastcase had limited federal-case coverage. Lawyer Steven Schwartz needed precedent to oppose an airline's motion to dismiss on statute-of-limitations grounds, heard about ChatGPT in the news, and mistook it for a "super search engine."

**Result**: The brief cited six judicial decisions with quotes and citations — all invented by ChatGPT. Opposing counsel couldn't locate any of them; the court examined every one and sanctioned the lawyers $5,000, finding bad faith in acts of "certifying that the fake cases were real." The judge noted the lawyers "abandoned their responsibility to verify." The handbook files it under the top failure pattern: no human verification before filing.

*Source: U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York, Mata v. Avianca, Inc. (Doc. 54, 2023-06-22) (judicial document)*
