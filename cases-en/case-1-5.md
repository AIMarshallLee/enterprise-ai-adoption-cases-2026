# Case 1.5 (Australia, FAILURE CASE): Employee Training & Capability Building — Treasury's Copilot trial: high expectations and insufficient training left usage below expectations

**Industry / scenario**: (Government body) The Australian Government's Department of the Treasury; a trial of staff using a generative AI office assistant (Microsoft 365 Copilot).

**Pain point**: Through a 14-week trial assessed by an independent evaluator, Treasury wanted to find out how much a generative AI office assistant could actually contribute to the department's day-to-day work.

**Why it failed**: The government's evaluation summary report states that the technical deployment went smoothly, but the time available for training and for learning to use Copilot was limited; participants had high expectations of the product that were not met; unrealistically high expectations at the start of the trial may have been one of the reasons for low usage, and some staff stopped using it because they were disappointed with the product's performance; and Copilot was not suited to more complex tasks, mainly because of limitations of the product itself. Throughout the trial, participants frequently asked for more targeted education and training.

**Outcome**: The trial ran for 14 weeks (2024-05-20 to 2024-08-23), with 218 staff participating. Overall usage was lower than expected: most participants used it only 2–3 times a week or less, while 22% used it 4–5 times a week or more. According to the government evaluation page, nearly two-thirds of users considered it suitable for, and helpful with, basic administrative work. According to Information Age, before the trial three-quarters of participants expected Copilot to help with some of their tasks, but during the trial only 38% felt it did; before the trial 15% expected it to help with most tasks, and by the end only 2% did (these figures do not appear in the body of the government report and are media-reported).

**Lessons**: The evaluation report's recommendations for future generative AI rollouts include: set out clear, specific use cases and allocate licences to staff who can demonstrate they are likely to benefit; roll out new products in phases in future; and assess how much should be invested in education and training. Buying a tool does not mean staff will use it — use cases and training need to be designed in advance.

*Sources: Australian Treasury, "Evaluation of a trial of generative AI (Copilot) in The Treasury" evaluation page (2025-02); Australian Centre for Evaluation, "Evaluation of a trial of generative artificial intelligence (Copilot) in the Department of the Treasury" summary report and full report (2025-02) (government); Information Age (ACS), "Treasury releases Microsoft Copilot trial results" (2025-02-13) (media report; used only for the two figure pairs 75%→38% and 15%→2%)*
