# Case 24.6 (UK/Hong Kong, FAILURE CASE): Engineering — Arup's Hong Kong staff lost HK$200M to an AI deepfake video-conference scam

**Industry / scenario**: (Large enterprise: 18,500 employees across 34 offices globally) Arup, the London-headquartered engineering consultancy. Scenario: financial payment approval. Note: this is a case of staff being scammed with AI deepfakes — a failure of internal controls against AI-enabled fraud, not a failed Arup AI project.

**Pain point / what happened**: The incident hit the finance payment process: a Hong Kong finance employee received an email supposedly from the UK office proposing a "secret transaction," then a video call with deepfaked likenesses of the CFO and colleagues. Believing the meeting was real, the employee made 15 transfers totaling ~HK$200M (~US$25.6M).

**Result**: Hong Kong police confirmed the deepfake nature of the call; the case became the canonical example of AI-enabled social engineering at scale. The handbook files it under "novel AI-specific security risks" — alongside the coding agent that deleted a production database — as evidence that AI risk governance must cover fraud and agent safety, not just output quality.

*Sources: Hong Kong police statements and international press coverage (CNN, The Guardian, etc., Jan–Feb 2024); Arup later confirmed the incident (media-reported)*
