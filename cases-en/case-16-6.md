# Case 16.6 (Canada, FAILURE CASE): Transport & Aviation — Air Canada's chatbot gives wrong information on its bereavement-fare policy, and the airline is ordered to pay compensation

**Industry / scenario**: (Size undisclosed: headcount not disclosed in the source) Airline / customer-service chatbot on the official website

**Pain point**: Air Canada offers a customer-service chatbot on its website to provide information to people visiting the site; the decision notes that Air Canada did not provide any information about the nature of this chatbot. Passenger Moffatt used it on the day his grandmother died, when he went to Air Canada's website to look up and book flights.

**Why it failed**: The website chatbot told Moffatt that, if he had already travelled, he could submit an application for the reduced bereavement fare within 90 days of the ticket being issued. Moffatt later learned from an Air Canada employee that the airline does not accept retroactive applications; Air Canada's "Bereavement travel" webpage also states that the policy does not apply to requests made after travel has been completed. Air Canada argued that it should not be liable for information provided by the chatbot. The tribunal member found that this amounted to treating the chatbot as "a separate legal entity that is responsible for its own actions," called it a "remarkable submission," and rejected it. The decision notes that Air Canada did not provide any information about the nature of the chatbot.

**Outcome**: On February 14, 2024, the British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal found that Air Canada had committed negligent misrepresentation and ordered it to pay a total of CAD 812.02, consisting of CAD 650.88 in damages, CAD 36.14 in pre-judgment interest and CAD 125 in tribunal fees.

**Lessons**: The decision states: "It should be obvious to Air Canada that it is responsible for all the information on its website." When customer-facing AI answers questions about policies, prices or refunds, its answers must match the official policy text; a business cannot escape liability by saying it was the bot that said it.

*Sources: Civil Resolution Tribunal, "Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149" (2024-02-14, tribunal member Christopher C. Rivers) (court/tribunal document)*
