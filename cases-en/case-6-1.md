# Case 6.1 (United States): Logistics — UPS plans delivery sequences with its ORION route optimization system, projecting 100 million fewer miles driven a year

**Industry / scenario**: (Large enterprise: at full deployment, ORION covers 55,000 U.S. drivers) U.S. parcel carrier UPS; daily route planning for last-mile pickup and delivery.

**Pain point**: UPS has more than a century of pickup-and-delivery tradition and needed to combine its established practices with new technology. Route optimization algorithms developed early on by its operations research team worked in the lab but proved hard to put into practice in real operations.

**AI solution**: UPS developed the ORION route optimization system on its Package Flow Technology (PFT) data platform (an operations research optimization algorithm, not generative AI or machine learning), and began deploying it in 2012 after about 10 years of R&D. Using customer, driver and vehicle data together with all of the day's scheduled pickup and delivery stops, the system calculates the most efficient route; for a 120-stop route, for example, it analyzes more than 200,000 options. Each morning, the system gives drivers the optimal delivery sequence for their packages.

**Results**: As of December 2015, more than 35,000 of 55,000 U.S. drivers were using ORION, with full deployment planned by the end of 2016, at which point drivers would serve an average of 160 customers a day. Each deployed route averages 6–8 fewer miles driven. UPS expects that at full deployment it will cut 100 million miles driven, save 10 million gallons of fuel and reduce emissions by 100,000 metric tons of carbon dioxide each year, saving USD 300 million–400 million a year; as of December 2015 it had already saved more than USD 320 million, and the full deployment cost of the project was about USD 250 million. (Basis: company self-reported — UPS press release and INFORMS Edelman Prize materials; the annual effects are all projected figures. ORION is an operations research optimization algorithm, not machine learning or generative AI.)

**Who this fits**: Parcel, retail delivery and field-service companies with large fleets and driver workforces and many stops a day; the prerequisite is first having a data platform for stops, vehicles and orders.
