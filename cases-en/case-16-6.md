# Case 16.6 (Canada, FAILURE CASE): Airlines — Air Canada's chatbot gave wrong bereavement-fare information and the airline was held liable

**Industry / scenario**: (Size undisclosed: headcount not given in sources) Airline / website customer-service chatbot.

**Pain point**: Air Canada ran a support chatbot on its website; the tribunal ruling noted the airline provided no information about the bot's nature or limitations. Passenger Jake Moffatt used it on the Air Canada site while booking a flight on the day his grandmother died.

**Result**: The chatbot gave incorrect information about the bereavement fare policy (stating refunds could be sought within 90 days — which the airline later said did not apply). The Civil Resolution Tribunal held Air Canada liable for the difference, awarding CAD 812.02, rejecting the argument that the chatbot was "a separate legal entity that is responsible for its own words." The handbook files this under "no human fallback on external answers or automated decisions."

*Source: Civil Resolution Tribunal, Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149 (2024-02-14, adjudicator Christopher C. Rivers) (judicial document)*
